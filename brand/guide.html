The design language. A workbench: everything on it is real, made by the person showing it to you, and can be taken apart.

Version 0.1 · November 2026 · draft
01

The idea

  1. Show the real thing

    Real CAD models, real screenshots, real photos, Marcus's own words. A bad stand-in is worse than a gap.

  2. Draw it in

    Nothing just appears. Lines are stroked, shapes fill, objects arrive from somewhere.

  3. Cut it open

    The logo is a section view. The site shows how things work, mistakes included.

  4. Say it plainly

    Dry, specific, first person. The engineering references are real ones, used correctly.

05

Line

Each line means one thing.

Edge, 3 px. The outline of a real thing. Rule, 1.5 px. The top of a list or a section. Dotted, 1 px. Between rows. Chain line, orange. A centre line. At most one on a screen.

The mat grid: cream lines every 20 px at 8% and every 100 px at 20%.

07

Motion

Drawn in, never popped in.

Eases, by job

Flow · lines, wipes
Draw · one stroke
Move · arriving
Part · set pieces

Stroked by its own length

  • Stroke lines, wipe fills, write text then ink it.
  • Overlap: the next thing starts before the last ends.
  • 200 ms feedback, 450 ms small moves, 900 ms a stroke, 1.6 s a camera move.
  • Pin scroll only on wide screens, only for a page's main story.
  • With reduced motion or no JavaScript, everything is visible as a plain page.
  • Fading up from nothing as the main effect.
  • Pops, bounces, overshoot.
  • Still images cross-fading in place of animation.
  • Sideways-scrolling carousels.
  • Pinned scrolling on phones.
08

3D and voice

3D

  • Real geometry first, one file per part, in assembled coordinates.
  • Millimetres. Objects are their true size against each other.
  • One warm key light, a hemisphere fill, a neutral room for reflections.
  • A section is a clipping plane. Cut faces are flat orange on printed parts.
  • A stand-in must look right at the size shown, or it goes.

Voice

  • First person, dry, specific. British English, metric.
  • Labels are short capitals in mono.
  • Drawing notes where they are true: DO NOT SCALE, IF IN DOUBT, ASK.
  • Never invent facts, numbers or steps.
  • No exclamation marks, no marketing adjectives.
